================================================================================
EEG PIPELINE OUTPUT GUIDE
================================================================================
This document explains all graphs, CSVs, and text files produced by the EEG
pipeline, what each shows, and what can be deduced from them.

================================================================================
INPUT DATA
================================================================================
  The pipeline reads raw EEG from CSV files (ALAS Muse / similar recordings).

  Format:
    - Columns: Time, MV1, MV2, MV3, MV4
    - Time: Unix timestamp (seconds) for each sample
    - MV1, MV2, MV3, MV4: Voltage in microvolts (µV) for each channel
    - Sampling: Variable in raw file; pipeline resamples to 250 Hz uniformly

  Channel roles:
    MV1, MV3  - Reference channels
    MV2, MV4  - Active/measurement channels (scalp)

  File naming: ALAS_Recording01_P01_Dyad01_TaskNN.csv (or similar)
  Location: TestEEGData/Dyad01/P01/ (or P02/, etc.)

================================================================================
OUTPUT LOCATION
================================================================================
Outputs are saved in TestEEGData/Dyad01/P01Results/<TaskNN>/ (e.g. Task01,
Task02, Task03, Task04). Each task folder contains the results for that
recording. The pipeline state suffix (e.g. _default, _no_reference) indicates
how the data was processed.

================================================================================
PIPELINE STATES (suffixes in filenames)
================================================================================
  _default           - Auto re-referencing with quality check (recommended)
  _no_reference      - Raw MV2/MV4 only; no subtraction of reference channels
  _forced_reference  - Always re-reference (MV2-MV1, MV4-MV3) regardless of
                       reference quality
  _no_quality_check  - Auto re-reference but skip quality checks

================================================================================
WHAT IS RE-REFERENCING?
================================================================================

  EEG measures voltage *between* two electrodes. The raw recording has four
  channels: MV1, MV2, MV3, MV4. MV1 and MV3 are "reference" electrodes (often
  placed at electrically quiet sites like mastoid or ear). MV2 and MV4 are the
  "active" or measurement electrodes over scalp regions of interest.

  RE-REFERENCING means subtracting the reference from the measurement:
    EEG1 = MV2 - MV1
    EEG2 = MV4 - MV3

  WHY IT HELPS:
    - Removes common noise: Both the reference and measurement pick up the same
      environmental noise (power line, cable movement, body drift). Subtracting
      the reference cancels out that shared component, leaving a cleaner signal
      that better reflects brain activity at the measurement site.
    - Reduces DC drift: Slow baseline shifts that affect both electrodes are
      removed by the subtraction.
    - Standard practice: Most EEG analysis uses a referenced signal rather than
      raw single-electrode voltage.

  WHEN IT'S SKIPPED (quality check):
    If the reference channels (MV1, MV3) are noisy, contaminated, or have large
    artifacts (e.g. movement, loose contact), subtracting them can *inject* that
    noise into the derived EEG instead of removing it. The pipeline checks
    reference quality (variance and peak-to-peak) and skips re-referencing when
    the reference is too noisy. In that case, raw MV2 and MV4 are used instead.

  WITHOUT RE-REFERENCING (no_reference mode):
    The signal still contains common-mode noise and drift. It may be noisier
    but avoids the risk of subtracting a bad reference. Use when references are
    known to be unreliable.

================================================================================
1. BAND POWER GRAPH (band_power_<state>.png)
================================================================================

WHAT IT SHOWS:
  A 7-panel figure with time on the x-axis (seconds) and the following panels:

  Panel 1: Delta (1-4 Hz)     - Power in µV²/Hz over time
  Panel 2: Theta (4-8 Hz)     - Power in µV²/Hz over time
  Panel 3: Alpha (8-13 Hz)    - Power in µV²/Hz over time
  Panel 4: Beta (13-30 Hz)   - Power in µV²/Hz over time
  Panel 5: Gamma (30-50 Hz)  - Power in µV²/Hz over time
  Panel 6: Beta/Alpha ratio  - Dimensionless ratio over time
  Panel 7: Theta/Beta ratio  - Dimensionless ratio over time

  Each band power is computed from EEG channel 1 (MV2, or MV2-MV1 when
  re-referenced) using a 2-second sliding window with 1-second step (50%
  overlap). Welch's method is used for the power spectral density.

WHAT CAN BE DEDUCED:
  - Band power panels: Which frequency bands dominate at each moment. For
    example, high alpha during eyes-closed rest, or increased beta during
    focused tasks.
  - Beta/Alpha ratio: Engagement vs. relaxation. Higher = more focused/alert;
    lower = more relaxed. The dashed line at 1.0 marks the crossover.
  - Theta/Beta ratio: Alertness/attention. Higher = more drowsy or inattentive;
    lower = more alert and engaged.
  - Temporal patterns: How brain activity changes across the recording (e.g.,
    transitions from rest to task, or fluctuations in attention).
  - Artifacts: Sudden spikes or sustained elevations may indicate movement,
    muscle activity, or electrode issues.

================================================================================
2. FILTERED EEG GRAPH (filtered_eeg_<state>.png)
================================================================================

WHAT IT SHOWS:
  A time-series plot of the raw EEG signal after processing:
  - Bandpass filtered 1-50 Hz (removes DC drift and high-frequency noise)
  - Optionally re-referenced (EEG1 = MV2-MV1, EEG2 = MV4-MV3)
  - Two channels: EEG 1 (from MV2) and EEG 2 (from MV4)
  - A 5-second window (10-15 seconds into the recording) for visual clarity

  Y-axis: Voltage in microvolts (µV)
  X-axis: Time in seconds

WHAT CAN BE DEDUCED:
  - Signal quality: Clean EEG shows smooth, oscillatory waves. Large spikes,
    flat lines, or very irregular patterns suggest artifacts (movement, loose
    electrodes, muscle).
  - Channel comparison: EEG 1 vs EEG 2 can reveal if one channel is noisier or
    if both track similar activity.
  - Dominant rhythms: Alpha (8-13 Hz) appears as ~10 waves per second;
    theta (4-8 Hz) as slower waves. Visual inspection can confirm band-power
    findings.

================================================================================
3. BAND POWER TIMESERIES CSV (band_power_timeseries_<state>.csv)
================================================================================

WHAT IT CONTAINS:
  - First line: Comment with pipeline_state
  - Columns: time_sec, Delta, Theta, Alpha, Beta, Gamma, beta_alpha_ratio,
             theta_beta_ratio
  - One row per 1-second step (centered on 2-second windows)
  - Band power values in µV²/Hz; ratios are dimensionless

WHAT CAN BE DEDUCED:
  - Full numeric data for all bands and ratios over time
  - Suitable for statistical analysis, correlation with task events, or
    export to other tools
  - Mean, std, and trends: Compute summary stats (e.g., mean Beta/Alpha during
    task vs. rest) for quantitative comparisons
  - Time-locked analysis: Align with task markers to study changes at specific
    moments (e.g., stimulus onset, response)

================================================================================
4. STREAM RATIOS TEXT FILE (stream_ratios_<state>.txt)
================================================================================

WHAT IT CONTAINS:
  - Pipeline state and input/output paths
  - Reference channel stats: MV1 and MV3 variance (uV²) and peak-to-peak (uV)
  - Re-referencing status: "applied" or "skipped" with reason if skipped
  - Per-second Beta/Alpha and Theta/Beta ratios (same as in the CSV)
  - Summary: mean, std, and last value for both ratios

WHAT CAN BE DEDUCED:
  - Reference quality: Low variance and pp indicate clean reference channels;
    high values may explain why re-referencing was skipped.
  - Quick ratio summary: Mean and std give an overview of engagement and
    alertness for the recording.
  - Human-readable log of processing and results for that task.

================================================================================
5. REFERENCE CHANNEL STATS (uV² and uV)
================================================================================

  MV1, MV3: Reference channels. The pipeline reports:
    - var (variance, uV²): Variability of the reference. High = noisy.
    - pp (peak-to-peak, uV): Max minus min voltage. High = large swings.

  Thresholds (when quality check is on):
    - var > 1000 uV²  → re-referencing skipped
    - pp > 200 uV     → re-referencing skipped

  Clean references typically have var in tens to low hundreds and pp < 100 µV.

================================================================================
UNITS QUICK REFERENCE
================================================================================
  µV (microvolt)      - Voltage; used for EEG amplitude, filtered signal, pp
  µV²/Hz              - Power spectral density; used for band power (Delta,
                        Theta, Alpha, Beta, Gamma)
  uV² (microvolt²)    - Variance; used for reference channel variability
  (ratio)             - Dimensionless; Beta/Alpha and Theta/Beta have no units

================================================================================
6. TYPICAL INTERPRETATIONS
================================================================================

  Beta/Alpha ratio:
    < 1   → More alpha than beta; relaxed, idle, or eyes-closed
    ≈ 1   → Balanced
    > 1   → More beta than alpha; focused, alert, engaged

  Theta/Beta ratio:
    Higher → More theta relative to beta; drowsy, inattentive, or default state
    Lower  → More beta relative to theta; alert, attentive, engaged

  Band power (µV²/Hz):
    - Delta (1-4 Hz): Often elevated in sleep or deep relaxation
    - Theta (4-8 Hz): Drowsiness, meditation, or cognitive load in some tasks
    - Alpha (8-13 Hz): Relaxed wakefulness, eyes closed; decreases with engagement
    - Beta (13-30 Hz): Active thinking, focus, alertness
    - Gamma (30-50 Hz): Higher-order processing; more variable and noisier

  Best practice: Compare within the same person (task vs. rest, or different
  tasks) rather than absolute values. Trends and condition differences are
  more informative than single numbers.

================================================================================
CAVEATS AND LIMITATIONS
================================================================================
  - Single channel: Band power and ratios are computed from EEG channel 1
    (MV2, or MV2-MV1 when re-referenced) only. EEG 2 (MV4) is plotted in the
    filtered EEG graph but not used for band power. Results reflect activity
    at one scalp location.
  - Window size: 2-second sliding windows with 1-second step. Shorter windows
    give poorer frequency resolution; longer windows smooth over rapid changes.
  - Electrode placement: Interpretation depends on where MV2 and MV4 are
    placed. Different scalp regions have different typical band profiles.
  - Ratios are descriptive, not diagnostic: Beta/Alpha and Theta/Beta are
    useful for comparing conditions but should not be used alone for clinical
    or diagnostic conclusions.
  - Artifacts: Movement, muscle, eye blinks, and loose electrodes can
    contaminate band power. Inspect the filtered EEG graph for obvious
    artifacts before trusting band-power or ratio values.
